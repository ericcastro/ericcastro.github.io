For a while now I have been using a small app I made to fix one very specific annoyance in my music life: DJ sets are great, but listening history treats them like one giant anonymous blob.

If I listen to an Amelie Lens set for two hours, Last.fm only knows I listened to a YouTube video. It doesn't know the tracks. It doesn't know the artists. It doesn't know anything useful, which is tragic for someone who has been compulsively scrobbling music for more than twenty years and still thinks Last.fm is one of the best ideas the internet ever had.

So I built **DJ Scrobbler**.

It lets me search for DJ sets, pulls tracklists from places like 1001Tracklists when available, plays the set, and scrobbles the individual tracks to Last.fm as they come in. It also keeps favorites, history, playback progress, themes, and the tiny amount of desktop-app ceremony that makes it feel like something I actually want to keep open.

<p class="post-image-frame"><img src="/images/projects/dj-scrobbler-neon-night.png" alt="DJ Scrobbler desktop app showing a YouTube DJ set, tracklist, and Last.fm scrobbling controls." /></p>

This release is the first one I am comfortable telling people to try. Not "this is a funny local prototype that works on my machine if you know which wires not to touch", but a real public beta that should be usable on macOS, Windows, and Linux.

It is still unsigned, because paying the platform tax before knowing if anyone besides me cares would be a bit too responsible and therefore suspicious. So yes, macOS and Windows may complain before opening it. The app is open source, the builds happen on GitHub, and the warnings are mostly the usual punishment for distributing software outside the blessed stores.

The funny part is that this also became one of the clearest examples of how I currently like working with AI coding tools. DJ Scrobbler was heavily AI-assisted, but not in the "press button, receive startup" fantasy way. More like: I knew exactly the itch, the shape, the taste, the broken little edge cases, and then I kept steering until the thing stopped feeling like a demo and started feeling like software.

It's been a long time since I made something for public consumption. And since nostalgia is probably the most repeated word around here, yes, this brings back lovely memories from my old MewSeek days, when I made something actually useful for people between 2008 and 2014, unsurprisingly also orbiting around music. My expectations may be a bit high, and I can't wait to get this to v1.0.

In the meantime, if you listen to a lot of DJ sets, whether you care about Last.fm or not, this should still be for you. Hope you like it. This is probably the moment when I'll be most open to receiving suggestions, aside from bug reports, which I also very much encourage you to send my way.
